## What it adds to COA

COA derives the propagation methods. Etter surveys the models built on them and the rest of the sonar modeling chain, with references to the literature on each. Use it for:

- the oceanography models need: physical and chemical properties, sound speed and its empirical equations, boundaries, dynamic features (Ch. 2)
- observed propagation phenomena: sea-surface and sea-floor boundaries, attenuation and absorption, surface ducts, the deep sound channel, convergence zones, shallow-water ducts, the Arctic half-channel, coherence (Ch. 3)
- the propagation model families, each with a summary of the numerical models available: ray theory, normal modes, multipath expansion, fast field and parabolic equation (Ch. 4); surface-duct, shallow-water and Arctic models (Ch. 5)
- special applications: stochastic and broadband modeling, matched field processing, 3D modeling, fronts, eddies and internal waves, tomography, geoacoustic inversion, marine mammal protection (Ch. 6)
- noise and reverberation, each as observations and physical models, then mathematical models (Ch. 7-10)
- sonar equations and sonar performance models (Ch. 11)
- model evaluation, including analytical benchmark solutions and quantitative accuracy assessment (Ch. 12), and simulation (Ch. 13)
- the acronyms of models and programs (App. A) and a glossary (App. B)

Chapter 5 is printed with the same title as Chapter 4, "Propagation II: Mathematical Models"; it continues the propagation models.
