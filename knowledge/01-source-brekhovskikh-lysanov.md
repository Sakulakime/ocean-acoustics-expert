## What it adds to COA

A compact classical treatment of ocean-acoustic theory, analytical where COA is computational. Use it for:

- the ocean as an acoustic medium: sound-velocity profiles and the propagation conditions they create, absorption, ocean variability (fronts, eddies, internal waves, fine structure, turbulence), the surface, bubbles, deep-scattering layers, the bottom, ambient noise, intrathermocline lenses (Ch. 1)
- ray theory: refraction, the focusing factor and caustics, three-dimensional refraction, Snell's law for a range-dependent ocean, tomography, weakly divergent ray bundles (Ch. 2)
- reflection and transmission at the surface and bottom: plane waves on liquid, layered and solid bottoms (Ch. 3); a point source, the reflected wave and the lateral wave (Ch. 4)
- shallow water: image sources, normal modes, how the representations relate, the averaged decay law (Ch. 5)
- the underwater sound channel: trapping, the canonical channel, convergence zones, mode sums, WKB modes and the phase integral, spatial periods of interference (Ch. 6)
- range-dependent waveguides: the reference-waveguide method, the adiabatic approximation and ray invariant with its validity conditions, coupled modes, horizontal rays, the parabolic equation (Ch. 7); antiwaveguides (Ch. 8)
- scattering at rough surfaces: the Rayleigh parameter, small perturbation, the tangent-plane method, two-scale roughness, rough surface channels, fore-reverberation, the small-slope approximation (Ch. 9)
- the random ocean: amplitude and phase fluctuations, scattering by random inhomogeneities, phase fluctuations due to internal waves, fluctuations in multipath propagation (Ch. 10); scattering and absorption by bubbles (Ch. 11)
