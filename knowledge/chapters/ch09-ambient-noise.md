# COA Chapter 9 - Ambient Noise

## Summary
This chapter models ambient noise as a propagation problem, with emphasis on surface-generated noise, cross-spectral density, modal and wavenumber descriptions, noise-correlation physics, and three-dimensional noise fields. It is the main COA bridge from propagation theory to passive sensing backgrounds and noise-based Green's-function recovery.

## Core problem
How to represent the ocean noise field quantitatively enough to predict both its masking effect on sensing systems and the propagation information embedded in its spatial and temporal correlations.

## Method-selection snapshot
- Best when the question concerns passive background structure, sensor-to-sensor noise correlation, vertical or horizontal coherence, or Green's-function recovery from noise.
- Best when ambient noise must be tied back to waveguide physics rather than treated as an abstract interference term.
- Weak when the dominant issue is deterministic source propagation rather than stochastic distributed sources.

## Major subsections

### Surface noise in a stratified ocean
The chapter begins by treating ambient noise as the field generated by a distributed sheet of surface sources in a stratified ocean waveguide.

**Key equations and topics**
- Surface-distributed source model
- Cross-spectral density of the noise field
- Dependence on Green's functions between source and receiver points
- Sensor-to-sensor correlation as the core observable
- Relation between intensity and diagonal entries of the correlation matrix

**Physical interpretation**
- Ambient noise is not only a scalar noise level; it is a spatially structured random field.
- The quantity that matters operationally is often the cross-spectral density between receivers, because that controls array response and coherence.

### Source statistics and the spatial distribution of noise sources
The next material makes the source model explicit by classifying the types of surface sources and their correlation structure.

**Key equations and topics**
- Monopole, dipole, and quadrupole source classes
- Correlated versus uncorrelated source fields
- Source directivity with respect to the vertical
- Dependence of the noise spectrum on source statistics

**Why it matters**
- Ambient-noise predictions depend as much on assumptions about distributed source physics as on the ocean environment.
- Changing source correlation or source multipole type changes the resulting spatial coherence of the received field.

### Wavenumber-integral representation of ambient noise
One major route is to express the cross-spectral density through the spectral representation already developed for layered propagation problems.

**Key equations and topics**
- Wavenumber representation of the Green's function
- Spectral integral form of the cross-spectral density
- Horizontal wavenumber dependence of noise coherence
- Efficient reuse of WI machinery for noise correlation functions

**Best-fit scenarios**
- Stratified, range-independent environments
- Layered media where spectral structure is the natural language
- Cases where one wants explicit separation of horizontal spectral contributions

### Normal-mode representation and modal coherence structure
The chapter also develops a modal picture of ambient noise, making waveguide structure and attenuation effects more interpretable.

**Key equations and topics**
- Normal-mode expansion of the noise field
- Coherence between modal contributions
- Role of attenuation in making the noise field finite
- Incoherent-sum approximations under common underwater conditions
- Relation between discrete and continuous spectral contributions

**Physical interpretation**
- The noise field inherits waveguide structure just as deterministic signals do.
- Modal language explains how vertical directionality, trapping, and attenuation shape passive-noise observations.

### Homogeneous halfspace and stratified-media limits
The chapter then uses limiting cases and stratified examples to show how the noise field changes across environments.

**Key equations and topics**
- Noise in a homogeneous halfspace
- Stratified-media noise fields
- Discrete versus continuous spectral components
- Vertical directionality and beamformer response
- Horizontal spatial correlation in different bottoms and SSPs

**Best-fit scenarios**
- Interpreting how bottom loss and sound-speed structure change passive backgrounds
- Comparing simple canonical models with more realistic waveguides
- Low-frequency shallow-water problems with strong bottom interaction

**Limitations**
- The field structure depends strongly on attenuation, bottom properties, and source assumptions
- Simple halfspace models can miss the waveguide partition between discrete and continuous spectra

### Extracting Green's functions from noise correlations
A major conceptual turn in the chapter is that ambient noise can be used not just as interference, but as a source of propagation information.

**Key equations and topics**
- Time-domain Green's functions from noise correlation functions
- Emergence of coherent wavefronts from stochastic backgrounds
- Passive recovery of propagation information from correlations
- Data examples of extracting wavefronts from noise

**Why it matters**
- This section links ambient-noise theory to passive imaging and wavefield reconstruction ideas.
- It shows that correlation structure can encode the underlying propagation physics, not merely obscure it.

### Surface noise in a three-dimensional ocean
The final major section generalizes ambient-noise modeling beyond 2D stratified settings to realistic 3D environments.

**Key equations and topics**
- Three-dimensional surface-noise modeling
- Adiabatic-mode treatment of 3D noise fields
- Simulated noise fields in 3D environments
- PE-based noise modeling
- Downslope and deep-ocean-basin noise fields

**Best-fit scenarios**
- Horizontal refraction or cross-range structure in passive environments
- Regional-scale noise-field modeling in realistic bathymetry
- Problems where 2D vertical-slice approximations miss key passive-noise behavior

**Limitations**
- 3D ambient-noise modeling inherits the cost and approximation burden of the underlying 3D propagation method
- Adiabatic and PE treatments must still be judged by their own validity regimes

## Research takeaways
- Ch. 9 treats ambient noise as a structured wave field, not just as a scalar penalty term in the sonar equation.
- The main modeling choice is often how to represent source statistics and waveguide structure together: WI, modes, or 3D approximations.
- Passive Green's-function recovery and array-level noise prediction both depend on getting the correlation physics right, not merely the mean level.
